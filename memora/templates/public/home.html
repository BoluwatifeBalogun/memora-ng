Why early matters

By the time it is obvious, the damage is done.

Early Alzheimer's looks like ordinary forgetfulness, and the structural changes are subtle and spread across many slices. Manual reading is slow and varies between readers. Treatment and planning work best when the disease is found at its earliest stage.

How it reads a scan

Space first. Then sequence.

A convolutional network looks at small neighbourhoods of one image. Memora looks across each whole slice, then across the whole series.

  1. 01
    {% for i in range(5) %}{% endfor %}

    Upload the series

    Drop the axial slices from one scan, or a single NIfTI volume. The study is tied to a patient record.

  2. 02
    {{ ui.icon('check') }}

    Check and prepare

    Each slice is confirmed to be a greyscale brain MRI, intensity is normalised, and the series is resampled to 16 ordered slices.

  3. 03
    {% for i in range(36) %}{% endfor %}

    Vision Transformer

    Every slice is cut into 196 patches. Self-attention relates each patch to every other, so distant regions inform each other.

  4. 04
    {% for i in range(8) %}{% endfor %}

    LSTM across slices

    A bidirectional LSTM reads the 16 slice vectors in order and keeps what matters from neighbouring slices.

  5. 05

    A result you can check

    Stage, confidence, class probabilities, the slices that weighed most and an attention map. Everything is saved to the patient's history and can be printed as a report.

A second reader. Never the final word.

Memora NG is a decision-support tool for healthcare professionals. It analyses MRI only. It does not replace clinical judgement, cognitive assessment, laboratory tests or other imaging, and it does not give a final medical diagnosis.

Questions

Before you sign in.

What do I upload?{{ ui.icon('plus') }}

The axial slices from one T1-weighted brain MRI as PNG, JPG, BMP or TIFF files named in order, or a single NIfTI volume (.nii or .nii.gz). Sixteen or more slices through the ventricles and hippocampus give the best context.

Why does it want several slices?{{ ui.icon('plus') }}

The LSTM learns how the picture changes from one slice to the next. A single slice is accepted, but the sequence part of the model then has nothing to compare, and the result is marked as limited context.

What does the attention map show?{{ ui.icon('plus') }}

The regions the Vision Transformer attended to most on the slices that carried the most weight. It shows where the model looked. It is not a lesion marker and should not be read as one.

Who can create accounts?{{ ui.icon('plus') }}

Only an administrator. Healthcare professionals receive their login from the administrator at their facility.

Where is patient data kept?{{ ui.icon('plus') }}

In the deployment's own MySQL database and storage. Passwords are stored as salted hashes, records are visible only to the clinician who owns them and to administrators, and sign-ins and exports are written to an activity log.

Does it replace the radiologist?{{ ui.icon('plus') }}

No. It gives a fast, consistent second read to support the specialist's decision.
